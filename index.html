The house rules

Six decks · blackjack pays 3:2 · dealer stands on all 17s · American hole card and blackjack peek · double on any first two cards, including after a split · one split (two hands maximum) · split aces receive one card each · late surrender on the original first two cards · insurance pays 2:1.

Cards come from a shuffled six-deck shoe, reshuffled between rounds once 75% has been dealt. Table limits are $1–$500, in $0.50 increments. Wagers and payouts use simulated chips.

Leaving the table

Finish your hand, then cash out whenever you choose. There is no required profit or minimum session length. A +$250 target is a personal stopping reminder, not casino etiquette or a mathematical advantage. You can change or disable it before dealing.

What the review measures

Each decision receives a narrative about your opening hand, the dealer’s upcard, visible card removals, your available actions and the money at risk. We preserve this information at the moment you act. For full-hand play, each decision is also compared with the action having the highest expected profit among your legal options in the standard model. The engine uses an infinite-deck (replacement) probability model, conditioned on the dealer having no blackjack after the peek. It includes doubles, splits and surrender. Insurance is evaluated separately using the actual proportion of ten-value cards still unknown before the peek. Next-card bust and finishing-total probabilities also use finite-shoe counts, including the unknown hole card and any failed peek. The complete-hand model is a transparent approximation to the six-deck game, not an exact remaining-shoe solver. Shoe-specific next-card facts give context but do not convert that baseline recommendation into an exact depleted-shoe optimum. Differences within 0.005 betting units are marked near-equal.

Dollar EV loss is estimated decision regret multiplied by the wager at that decision; it is not an explanation of your realized losses. Betting analysis describes risk and changes after losses. No bet progression makes a negative-expectation game profitable.
